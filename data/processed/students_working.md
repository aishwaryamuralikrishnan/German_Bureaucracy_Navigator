# Working during your studies

Three separate sets of rules apply at the same time, and each has its own limit: the immigration rules (how many days you may work), the social-insurance rules (when you keep the cheap student status) and the tax rules (how much is tax-free). They are explained in turn.

## 1. Immigration: how much a non-EU student may work

Students with a residence permit under § 16b AufenthG may work 140 full days or 280 half days per calendar year without needing permission from the Ausländerbehörde or the Federal Employment Agency (§ 16b Abs. 3 AufenthG, raised from 120/240 days on 1 March 2024). A half day is any day on which you work up to half the regular daily hours of the company (usually up to four hours); a full day is anything more. The days are counted per calendar year across all employers, and unused days do not carry over.

Additionally allowed without counting towards the 140 days: student assistant jobs at the university or a research institute (studentische Hilfskraft / HiWi), compulsory internships that are part of the degree, and self-employed or freelance work only if the Ausländerbehörde has explicitly permitted it (it is otherwise not allowed).

Students in a preparatory language course or Studienkolleg may work only during the lecture-free period and only with permission. Working more than allowed is a breach of the residence conditions and can lead to fines or problems when extending the permit. EU/EEA and Swiss students have no such limit.

## 2. Social insurance: the 20-hour rule and the Werkstudent status

For social insurance, students who work no more than 20 hours per week during the lecture period keep the student privilege (Werkstudentenprivileg): the employer registers them as Werkstudent, and no health, care or unemployment insurance contributions are due on the wage. Only pension insurance is paid: 9.3 % employee share (the employer pays the other 9.3 %). The student continues to pay the cheap student health insurance separately.

Exceptions to the 20 hours: you may work more in the evenings, at night, at weekends and during the lecture-free period (semester break), as long as the total does not exceed 26 weeks (182 days) of more-than-20-hours work within a year. If you exceed the limits, you lose the Werkstudent status and are treated as a normal employee with full contributions (about 20 % of gross).

For non-EU students the 20-hour rule and the 140-day rule apply simultaneously: a typical Werkstudent contract of 18–20 hours over two or three days per week uses up half days or full days accordingly, so plan the calendar-year total.

## 3. Minijob

A Minijob is employment with earnings of at most EUR 603 per month (EUR 7,236 per year) in 2026; the limit is tied to the minimum wage of EUR 13.90 per hour, which corresponds to about 43 hours per month. Minijob wages are free of income tax for the employee (the employer pays a 2 % flat tax) and free of social contributions except a small pension contribution, from which you can opt out. A Minijob does not affect student health insurance. Immigration-wise the days still count towards the 140/280 days.

## 4. Taxes

Wages above the Minijob limit are subject to wage tax via your Steuer-ID in tax class I (or class VI for a second job). Because the tax-free basic allowance is EUR 12,348 in 2026, most students pay little or no income tax over the year; tax withheld in months with higher earnings is refunded when you file a tax return (Einkommensteuererklärung) via ELSTER — usually worthwhile for students. Keep payslips (Lohnabrechnung) and the annual wage-tax certificate (Lohnsteuerbescheinigung) from each employer.

## 5. Documents your employer needs

Steuer-ID, health insurance fund and membership number, social security number (Sozialversicherungsnummer — issued automatically at the first job, or requested from the pension insurance via the health fund), a current enrolment certificate (Immatrikulationsbescheinigung) each semester to prove student status, your residence permit showing the work permission text ("Beschäftigung bis zu 140 Tage/280 halbe Tage im Jahr … erlaubt"), and a German bank account for the salary.

## 6. Internships

A compulsory internship listed in the study regulations is not counted towards the 140 days and is exempt from social insurance regardless of hours; a voluntary internship counts like any other job. Interns in voluntary internships longer than three months must be paid at least the minimum wage.
