# EU Blue Card (Blaue Karte EU), § 18g AufenthG

## Purpose

The EU Blue Card is a residence title for non-EU academics and comparably qualified professionals who have a concrete job offer in Germany. It was reformed by the Skilled Immigration Act (Fachkräfteeinwanderungsgesetz) in November 2023, which lowered the salary thresholds and widened eligibility.

## Requirements

1. A German university degree, a recognised foreign degree, or a foreign degree comparable to a German one (check the anabin database or obtain a Zeugnisbewertung from the ZAB).
2. A binding job offer or employment contract for at least six months that matches the qualification.
3. A gross annual salary of at least the applicable threshold (see below).
4. For regulated professions (for example doctors), the professional licence (Approbation / Berufsausübungserlaubnis).

IT specialists without a degree can also obtain a Blue Card if they have at least three years of comparable professional experience gained within the last seven years and meet the reduced salary threshold.

## Salary thresholds

The thresholds are derived from the pension insurance contribution ceiling and change every January.

- General threshold: 50 % of the annual contribution assessment ceiling of the statutory pension insurance. For 2026 this is EUR 50,700 gross per year (2025: EUR 48,300; 2024: EUR 45,300).
- Reduced threshold: 45.3 % of the ceiling. For 2026 this is EUR 45,934.20 gross per year (2025: EUR 43,759.80; 2024: EUR 41,041.80). It applies to shortage occupations (Engpassberufe such as STEM professions, IT, medicine, nursing, teaching, engineering, veterinary medicine and certain management roles), to graduates whose degree is not more than three years old, and to IT specialists without a degree. For the reduced threshold the Federal Employment Agency (Bundesagentur für Arbeit) must approve the employment.

## Duration and permanent residence

The Blue Card is issued for the duration of the employment contract plus three months, up to a maximum of four years. Holders can apply for a permanent settlement permit (Niederlassungserlaubnis) after 27 months of Blue Card employment with pension contributions, or after 21 months with German language skills at level B1.

## Changing jobs

During the first twelve months, a change of employer must be reported to the Ausländerbehörde, which can object within 30 days. After twelve months the job can be changed freely as long as the Blue Card conditions are still met. Unemployment of up to three months does not end the Blue Card, but it must be reported.

## Family

Spouses and minor children can join the Blue Card holder without proving German language skills and receive immediate unrestricted access to the labour market. Sufficient living space and income must be shown.

## Mobility within the EU

After twelve months in Germany, Blue Card holders can move to another EU member state to take up qualified employment there under simplified rules (short-term mobility for business trips of up to 90 days is possible from day one).

## Application

If you are abroad, apply for a national visa for the purpose of the Blue Card at the German embassy or consulate. If you are already in Germany with a residence title that allows it, apply at the Ausländerbehörde. Typical documents: passport, employment contract, degree certificate and recognition evidence, CV, proof of health insurance, biometric photo, the completed application form and the "Erklärung zum Beschäftigungsverhältnis" form filled in by the employer.
