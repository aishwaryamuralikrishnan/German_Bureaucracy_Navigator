# Proof of financing and the blocked account (Sperrkonto)

## Why it is required

For the student visa and later for the student residence permit (§ 16b AufenthG) you must prove that your living costs for the next year are covered without public funds. The required amount follows the BAföG maximum rate and is set by the Federal Foreign Office.

## The required amount

For 2026 the amount is EUR 992 per month, i.e. EUR 11,904 for twelve months (unchanged since September 2024; before that EUR 934 per month). Embassies may ask for the full year even if the first permit is shorter, and the Ausländerbehörde checks the amount again at each extension for the remaining period of study.

## Ways to prove financing

1. Blocked account (Sperrkonto): the most common route for third-country students. You deposit the yearly amount before the visa interview and may withdraw at most EUR 992 per month after arrival; the rest stays blocked, which is what makes the proof credible.
2. Formal obligation letter (Verpflichtungserklärung): a person living in Germany with sufficient income signs a guarantee at their Ausländerbehörde to cover your costs. Free for you; the guarantor's income is checked.
3. Scholarship: an award letter from a recognised scholarship body (DAAD, Erasmus Mundus, a German university or foundation) stating at least the required monthly amount.
4. Parental income or bank guarantee: accepted by some embassies with detailed evidence; check the embassy's country-specific leaflet (Merkblatt Finanzierungsnachweis).

Combinations are possible, for example a partial scholarship plus a smaller blocked account covering the difference.

## The formal obligation letter (Verpflichtungserklärung) in detail

The Verpflichtungserklärung is a declaration under § 68 AufenthG in which a person living in Germany (the guarantor, "Verpflichtungsgeber") promises the Ausländerbehörde or the German mission abroad to cover the costs of your stay: living expenses, accommodation, health treatment and care, and if necessary the cost of your return journey. The guarantor signs it in person at the Ausländerbehörde of their own place of residence, shows proof of income (payslips, tax assessment) and pays a fee, currently EUR 29. The guarantor's freely disposable income must cover their own needs and yours.

Validity and how long it binds the guarantor: the declaration should be presented to the embassy or consulate reasonably soon after it was issued — the Federal Foreign Office and the Federal Ministry of the Interior state that no more than six months should lie between signing the declaration and the issue of the visa; older declarations are usually not accepted and a new one must be signed. Once you have entered Germany, the guarantor's liability for your living costs is limited by law to five years from entry (§ 68 Abs. 1 AufenthG). It ends earlier if the original purpose of your stay is replaced by another one and a new residence title is issued for it — for example when a student switches to an EU Blue Card or a skilled-worker permit after graduating. The liability does not end merely because the visa or the first residence permit expires; the authorities can reclaim public money spent on your maintenance from the guarantor during the five years.

For the extension of a student residence permit the Ausländerbehörde may accept a still-valid Verpflichtungserklärung as proof of financing, but many offices ask for a new declaration or a blocked-account statement at each extension. Ask the office which proof it accepts before the appointment.

## Opening a blocked account

Since 2022 the German banks have largely withdrawn from this business; blocked accounts are now offered by specialised providers approved by the Federal Foreign Office. Well-known providers in 2026 include Expatrio, Fintiba, Coracle and Care Concept, typically with an opening fee of about EUR 50–160 and monthly fees between EUR 0 and 10. Steps:

1. Open the account online with your passport and admission letter; identity is verified by video call or at a partner bank.
2. Transfer the yearly amount plus a small buffer for transfer fees (the embassy checks that the blocked amount is at least EUR 11,904).
3. Receive the blocking confirmation (Sperrbestätigung) and submit it with the visa application.
4. After arriving and opening a normal German current account, activate the account by providing that IBAN; the provider then pays out EUR 992 each month.

Choose a provider that also offers the health insurance bundle you want, that pays out quickly (some need the Anmeldung first) and that refunds the deposit if the visa is refused.

## Renewal and extension

When you extend the residence permit, the Ausländerbehörde again asks for proof of financing for the coming period — usually a new blocked-account statement, a scholarship letter or, once you work, employment contracts and payslips. A Werkstudent job can be counted towards the proof if the income is regular. Keep the yearly amount topped up before the extension appointment.

## When the visa is refused or you leave early

Money in a blocked account belongs to you. If the visa is refused, the account is closed and the balance returned against proof of refusal; providers charge a processing fee. If you leave Germany before the year ends, the remaining balance is paid out after deregistration (Abmeldung) or proof of departure.
