# Health insurance (Krankenversicherung)

## Mandatory for everyone

Health insurance is compulsory for every resident of Germany (§ 193 VVG, § 5 SGB V). Employers register you with the health insurance fund (Krankenkasse) you name; if you do not choose one, the employer registers you with a fund of its choice. You need proof of insurance for the residence permit and your employer needs the insurer's details for payroll.

## Statutory health insurance (Gesetzliche Krankenversicherung, GKV)

Employees with a gross salary up to the compulsory insurance threshold (Versicherungspflichtgrenze / Jahresarbeitsentgeltgrenze, EUR 77,400 in 2026) must be insured in the GKV. Above that threshold you may choose private insurance or remain voluntarily in the GKV.

Contributions in 2026: general rate 14.6 % of gross salary plus the fund's individual additional contribution (Zusatzbeitrag, on average 2.9 %), both split equally between employer and employee, up to the contribution ceiling of EUR 5,812.50 per month (EUR 69,750 per year). Long-term care insurance (Pflegeversicherung) is 3.6 %, also split, with an extra 0.6 % for childless members aged 23 and above and reductions for members with two or more children under 25.

Advantages: non-working spouses and children are covered free of charge (Familienversicherung); contributions depend on income, not on age or health; all funds offer the same core benefits, so you can choose by service, English-language support and extras. Popular choices among newcomers include TK, Barmer, DAK, AOK and hkk, but any of the roughly 90 funds is acceptable.

## Private health insurance (Private Krankenversicherung, PKV)

Available to employees above the threshold, self-employed people, civil servants and students. Premiums depend on age, health and the chosen tariff and are charged per person (no free family cover). The employer pays a subsidy of up to half of the premium, capped at the GKV maximum employer share. Switching back to the GKV later is difficult, especially after age 55, so the decision should be made carefully.

## Documents and steps after arrival

1. Choose a Krankenkasse online before or shortly after arrival; the application needs your passport, address (Meldebescheinigung once you have it) and employment contract.
2. You receive a membership certificate (Mitgliedsbescheinigung) for the employer and, a few weeks later, the electronic health card (elektronische Gesundheitskarte).
3. Your social security number (Sozialversicherungsnummer / Rentenversicherungsnummer) is issued by the pension insurance via the health fund when you first start work in Germany and arrives by post.

## Coverage gap on arrival

Travel or incoming insurance covers only the first weeks and is not accepted for a residence permit beyond the initial visa. Make sure statutory or full private cover starts on the first day of employment.
