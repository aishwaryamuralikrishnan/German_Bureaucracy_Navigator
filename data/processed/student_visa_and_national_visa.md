# From the visa in your passport to a residence permit

## What the visa in your passport is

Third-country nationals who come to Germany to study, work or join family usually enter with a national visa (category D) issued by the German embassy. It is normally valid for three to six months and states its purpose, for example "Studium § 16b AufenthG" or "Blaue Karte EU § 18g AufenthG". The visa is an entry document, not the final residence title: it gives you time to arrive, register your address and apply for the residence permit (Aufenthaltserlaubnis) at the local immigration office (Ausländerbehörde, in some cities called Migrationsamt or Landesamt für Einwanderung).

You do not "renew" the visa. Before it expires you apply to convert it into a residence permit for the same purpose. The residence permit is issued as an electronic card (eAT).

## Steps after arrival

1. Register your address (Anmeldung) within two weeks of moving in; the Ausländerbehörde needs the Meldebescheinigung.
2. Take out health insurance that is valid for the residence permit (statutory student insurance or a recognised private policy; incoming travel insurance is not sufficient for the permit).
3. Book the appointment at the Ausländerbehörde as early as possible; in university cities waiting times of several weeks are common. Applying before the visa expires is what matters: if the appointment is only available after the expiry date, the application counts as filed in time and you receive a Fiktionsbescheinigung (§ 81 Abs. 4 AufenthG) that keeps your stay lawful and allows you to continue studying or working under the previous conditions.
4. Attend the appointment with the documents below, pay the fee (up to EUR 100 for the first issue), and collect the eAT card a few weeks later.

## Documents for a student residence permit (§ 16b AufenthG)

- Passport with the national visa
- Biometric photo
- Completed application form (Antrag auf Erteilung eines Aufenthaltstitels)
- Meldebescheinigung
- Current enrolment certificate (Immatrikulationsbescheinigung) from the university
- Proof of health insurance
- Proof of financing for one year: blocked account (Sperrkonto) with the current required amount, a scholarship award letter, or a formal obligation letter (Verpflichtungserklärung). The required amount is adjusted regularly; in 2025 it was EUR 992 per month (EUR 11,904 per year).
- Rental contract or other proof of accommodation

The student permit is issued for at least one year and at most two years at a time and is extended as long as the studies progress properly. Students may work 140 full days or 280 half days per year (or 20 hours per week) alongside their studies.

## After graduation

Graduates of German universities can receive a residence permit to look for a job matching their qualification for up to 18 months (§ 20 Abs. 3 Nr. 1 AufenthG); during this period any employment is allowed. Once a qualified job is found, the permit is switched to the EU Blue Card or the skilled-worker permit (§ 18b AufenthG). Graduates of German universities can apply for a settlement permit after only two years of qualified employment.

## Switching from studies to work before graduation

A student residence permit can be changed into an EU Blue Card or skilled-worker permit if you already hold a recognised degree and a qualifying job offer; the Ausländerbehörde handles the change of purpose. Dropping out of studies must be reported and usually ends the student permit unless you switch to another purpose (for example vocational training or a work permit).

## Where to apply

The Ausländerbehörde responsible for your registered address. Examples: Landesamt für Einwanderung (Berlin), Kreisverwaltungsreferat (Munich), Hamburg Welcome Center or the district Ausländerbehörde (Hamburg), Migrationsamt (Rostock). Universities usually have an International Office or Welcome Center that helps with the first application.
