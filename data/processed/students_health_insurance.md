# Health insurance for students

## Why you need it before you can enrol

German universities require proof of health insurance before enrolment (Immatrikulation), and the Ausländerbehörde requires it for the student residence permit. Travel or incoming insurance bought for the visa application covers only the first weeks and is not accepted for enrolment or the permit beyond the initial visa period.

## Statutory student insurance (Krankenversicherung der Studenten, KVdS)

Students enrolled at a state or state-recognised university are compulsorily insured in the statutory system at a reduced student rate until the end of the semester in which they turn 30. The old limit of 14 subject semesters no longer applies; only the age limit remains. The rate is the same at every fund apart from the fund's individual additional contribution.

How the 2026 contribution is built (based on the BAföG requirement rate of EUR 855 per month):

- Health insurance: 10.22 % of EUR 855 = about EUR 87 per month, plus the fund's additional contribution (Zusatzbeitrag, on average 2.9 % = about EUR 25).
- Long-term care insurance (Pflegeversicherung): 3.6 % = about EUR 31 per month; students who are 23 or older and have no children pay 4.2 % = about EUR 36.
- Typical total: roughly EUR 130–150 per month including care insurance, depending on the fund. Funds with a low additional contribution are cheaper; service, English-language support and app quality are the other differences, because the benefits are identical by law.

Popular funds among international students include TK, Barmer, DAK, AOK and hkk; any statutory fund is acceptable. Apply online with your passport, admission letter (Zulassungsbescheid) and German address; the fund sends the university an electronic confirmation (Meldung zur Krankenversicherung der Studierenden) and mails your electronic health card (Gesundheitskarte).

## Who does not need German student insurance

- Students from EU/EEA countries and Switzerland with a European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) can stay insured at home; the German fund issues a confirmation of exemption for the university.
- Students from countries with a social security agreement covering health care (for example Bosnia and Herzegovina, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Serbia, Türkiye, Tunisia) may use their home insurance with the appropriate form.
- Students who are family-insured (Familienversicherung) through a spouse or parent in the German statutory system pay nothing until age 25 (children) or as a spouse without own income.

## Exemption in favour of private insurance

Within the first three months of compulsory insurance you may apply for an exemption (Befreiung von der Versicherungspflicht) and take out private insurance instead. This decision is binding for the whole period of study in Germany — you cannot return to the statutory system as a student later — so it is normally only sensible for short stays or for students who are already over 30. Private student tariffs from German insurers are accepted for enrolment; many foreign private policies are not.

## After 30 or after the 14th semester at some funds

From the age of 30 (or when studies end) the student rate stops. Options are voluntary statutory insurance at the minimum contribution of roughly EUR 230–260 per month in 2026, private insurance, or, if you take a job, insurance through the employer at the normal employee rate.

## Insurance and working

The student rate is linked to being a "proper student" (ordentlich Studierende): you must not work more than 20 hours per week during the lecture period (exceptions for evenings, weekends and semester breaks up to 26 weeks per year). Working more turns you into an ordinary employee for social insurance purposes, and the employer must register you at the full contribution rate. A Minijob (up to EUR 603 per month in 2026) does not affect the student insurance at all.

## Doctoral students

Doctoral candidates without a work contract are not compulsorily insured as students; they take voluntary statutory or private insurance. Those employed by the university as research assistants are insured through the employment.
