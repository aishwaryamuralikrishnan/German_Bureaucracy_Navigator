# Anmeldung (address registration)

## What it is and who must do it

Everyone who moves into a home in Germany must register the address with the local registration office (Meldebehörde, usually called Bürgeramt, Bürgerbüro or Einwohnermeldeamt). This applies to German citizens, EU citizens and third-country nationals alike, and to every move within Germany. The legal basis is § 17 Abs. 1 Bundesmeldegesetz (BMG).

## Deadline

You must register within two weeks (14 days) of moving in. Missing the deadline is an administrative offence (Ordnungswidrigkeit) under § 54 BMG and can be fined; in practice small delays are usually tolerated if you can show you tried to get an appointment, but you should book as early as possible because appointments in large cities are scarce.

If you stay in a hotel or short-term accommodation for less than three months (six months for a second home when you already have a registered main residence in Germany) you do not need to register yet. The two-week clock starts when you move into a home that is your actual residence.

## Documents you need

- Valid passport or national ID card (for every person being registered, including children).
- Wohnungsgeberbestätigung: a confirmation from the landlord (or the main tenant if you sublet) that you moved in, stating the address, move-in date and the landlord's name and address (§ 19 BMG). Your rental contract alone is not sufficient.
- Completed registration form (Anmeldeformular / Meldeschein). Many cities let you fill it in online before the appointment.
- For families: marriage certificate and birth certificates, ideally with a certified German translation, so that marital status and children can be recorded correctly.
- Visa or residence permit if you have one already (not required to register).

## What you receive

You get a Meldebescheinigung (registration certificate) on the spot. Keep it safe: banks, employers, health insurers and the immigration office ask for it. The registration also triggers two automatic processes:

- The Federal Central Tax Office (Bundeszentralamt für Steuern) sends your tax identification number (Steuer-ID) by post to the registered address, usually within two to four weeks.
- If you declare a religious affiliation that levies church tax, church tax (Kirchensteuer) is deducted from your salary.

## Fees and appointments

Registration itself is free of charge. Appointments are booked online through the city's service portal. In several large cities the Anmeldung can be completed fully online with a German eID (electronic ID card or eID card for EU/EEA citizens) via the "Elektronische Wohnsitzanmeldung" service; check your city portal for availability.

## Moving again or leaving Germany

Every move within Germany requires a new Anmeldung at the new address within two weeks; you do not need to deregister at the old one. If you leave Germany permanently you must deregister (Abmeldung) within two weeks of moving out, at the earliest one week before. Abmeldung is important for ending obligations such as the broadcasting fee (Rundfunkbeitrag) and health insurance.

## Consequences of registration

- Rundfunkbeitrag: every household pays the broadcasting fee; the fee office is informed by the registration office and will write to you.
- Tax class and church tax are derived from the data recorded at registration.
- Your registered address determines your responsible tax office (Finanzamt) and immigration office (Ausländerbehörde).
